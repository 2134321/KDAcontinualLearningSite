Kimi-Linear-48B-A3B-Base · hybrid linear attention · 20 KDA + 7 MLA layers

Does a transplanted KDA state carry the document it came from?

Possible evidence for KDA-based continual learning.

Prefill document A, keep only the 40 MiB KDA recurrent state, discard the KV and short-conv caches entirely, read a different document B, then ask about A. Three experiments, increasingly stringent.

Active research project — results are still being refined and further experiments may change the numbers below. Last updated 27 August 2026.

Why KDA, and why this should be possible at all

Kimi Linear interleaves two kinds of layer in a fixed 3:1 ratio: 20 KDA layers (linear attention, constant-size recurrent state) and 7 full-attention MLA layers (quadratic, KV cache that grows with context). KDA extends Gated DeltaNet with per-channel decay, giving each feature dimension its own forget rate.

The reason a transplant is even conceivable is what the KDA update actually is. Per token it applies

St = St−1Diag(at)(I − βtktkt⊤) + βtvtkt⊤

Two things happen per token. Diag(at) is KDA’s per-channel decay — the gate that separates it from an ungated delta rule, and from Gated DeltaNet’s single scalar forget gate. (This at is the architecture’s forget gate; it is unrelated to the scale factor α used elsewhere on this site.) What follows the decay is exactly one step of online gradient descent on ½∥S kt − vt∥², with βt as the learning rate. So the recurrent state is not a cache of past activations — it is a set of fast weights trained by gradient descent during the forward pass (Schlag, Irie & Schmidhuber, ICML 2021; see also Irie & Gershman 2025). The equation above follows the Kimi paper’s eq. 1, transposed into this page’s orientation for S.

If S is weights rather than cache, then S is a checkpoint. Which raises the question this experiment tests: can what the model learned during run A be carried into run B while the KV cache is thrown away entirely? If so, what carries forward between documents is constant-size — 40 MiB whether A was 1K tokens or 1M — however long the source document was. That is a claim about the handoff, not about the whole procedure: reading A still runs seven full-attention layers against a KV cache that grows with A, so nothing here makes the tested pipeline constant-memory end to end. For scale, the MLA cache these runs discard is 7 layers of keys [1, 32, T, 192] and values [1, 32, T, 128] — at bf16 that is 143 GB at 1M tokens. MLA's compressed latent form would be about 8 GB, but that is not what this implementation stores.

The answer measured here is yes at short lengths, and no at useful ones — with the state carried across unchanged. A 40 MiB state does carry a real, replicable trace of A across a full cache reset. But the trace thins as the document grows and is undetectable by 16K tokens, while the cost of carrying it holds steady, so past a few thousand tokens it costs more than it returns.

Read that as a floor, not a ceiling. Every number here is zero-shot. The model has never seen a recurrent state arriving from somewhere else, and nothing in its training prepared it for a KV cache that disagrees with its KDA state about how far into a document it is. That document-specific information survives such a transplant at all is the surprising part. A training recipe that included state resets, or transplanted states as an objective, could plausibly change the picture entirely — RWKV State Tuning already shows a learned initial state is a meaningful memory object in a related architecture. These experiments measure what a model does with a capability nobody trained it to have.

The headline so far

At a high level, it appears a small amount of information can be preserved, even without any training. It’s situational, and costly in some cases, but it suggests that this technique could be used as a foundation for broader continual learning.

All of it is zero-shot. Nothing in training exposed this model to a transplanted state, so these are measurements of an untrained capability rather than a limit on the architecture. These experiments show that some information survives a state transplant, but they do not yet establish a practically useful memory system. A next question is whether training explicitly for state transplants could make that retained information more useful and reliable.

What the claim rests on. Knowledge acquired from data A persists across a full cache reset and changes later behaviour — training-free, at inference time. It is specific to A rather than generic: the mismatched control stays flat at every scale tested.

What “limited” rules out. At the state’s natural scale the lift is about ten accuracy points over the mismatched control. Scaling the state roughly doubles that: in the α extension run warm − mismatched goes from +12.8 points at α = 1 to +29.5 points at α = 3, where 0.78 of items are correct against the mismatched control’s 0.49 — but the gain is bought rather than free: the same scaling turns a gain on the document actually in context into a loss, +0.89 nats at α = 1 against −0.81 at α = 3. And it is measured on synthetic planted facts in ~470-word documents, on one model.

What this does not show

  • At low α the state helps both documents. The lift on A is the point of the experiment; the surprise is that document B improves too. At the natural scale, carrying a state is worth +0.89 nats on B over cold (t(23) = +5.46, only 3 of 24 seeds worse), peaking near +0.97 at α ≈ 1.25. That lift is generic — warm and mismatched are nearly identical there, 18.27 against 18.20 — so it is not the state remembering A, it is the state being present at all. It decays as α rises, crosses zero near α = 2, and only then becomes a cost, which grows steeply: −0.81 at α = 3 and −6.88 at α = 12, where B accuracy finally leaves 1.00 (0.9931) — the first time in this project that measure has moved at all. So the gain on A is free at low α and paid for at high α. What this task still cannot speak to is harder capabilities it never exercises — multi-step reasoning, long-range dependency — where a subtler cost could hide.
  • “Document-specific” means fact-specific. A2 shares roughly 45% of its carrier sentences with A, because both sample the same filler pool. That makes the control tighter than a generic one — the effect cannot be explained by A’s state carrying shared register or vocabulary, since A2’s carries those too. But it also narrows the claim: what survives the transplant is information about the planted facts, over and above near-identical prose. An unrelated-prose A2 would support a broader claim on weaker control.
  • The depth effect runs backwards, and scaling sharpens it. Facts from the start of A — the ones the forget gates have had longest to decay — transfer best, at every scale where there is signal to measure. Clustered by seed (early third minus late third, one value per seed) that is +0.289 nats, p = 0.023 at α = 1, rising to +0.975, t(23) = +3.99, p = 0.0006, 20 of 24 seeds at α = 4. The shape is a sharp fall over the first third then a plateau, which looks like primacy rather than decay: facts written into a nearly-empty state may simply get a larger share of it. That is a guess, not a result — this experiment was not built to test it.
  • The ceiling is measured, but not at this length. An oracle run — A and B read as one uninterrupted sequence, so nothing is lost — puts the scaled transplant at about 2.7% of what simply having the document in context gives you. That was measured at 50,000 tokens. Experiment 1’s own 600-token ceiling has not been run, so the fraction preserved here is still unknown.

What this does and does not establish

  • At the natural scale, benefit falls and cost does not. Document-specificity is undetectable by 16K, while damage to the document in context sits at about −0.85 nats from 4K onward — flat across a 12× range of lengths. Past a few thousand tokens the transplant is worse than not doing it.
  • Scaling recovers it at 50,000 tokens, on both contrasts. The benefit first: warm − cold is +0.009 nats at α = 1 (p = 0.91) and +0.46 at α ≈ 5, t(23) = +4.15, p = 0.0004, 19 of 24 seeds. Document-specificity returns too, peaking slightly earlier: warm − mismatched is +0.38 at α = 3, t(23) = +3.94, p = 0.0007, 21 of 24 seeds. Estimates stay positive across α = 3–6 (raw p 0.0007, 0.027, 0.016, 0.020), but only α = 3 survives correcting for the eight settings swept — read that as one broad positive region, not four independent confirmations. Both contrasts are measured on 24 seeds against the ladder’s 8.
  • In decisions rather than confidence, the cost nearly vanishes. At 50,000 tokens, α = 4 gains +7.64 accuracy points on A-facts (t(23) = +2.74, p = 0.012) and loses 0.35 on B — one item in 288, and not distinguishable from zero. Net accuracy stays positive at every α tested. The two measures diverge because B’s margin sits about 17 nats above the decision boundary, so the −3.6 nats that α = 5 costs is a fifth of the way to flipping a single answer. Confidence lost and answers changed are different questions — and the same headroom that makes the second small is why B accuracy is a weak detector of damage on anything harder.
  • Cost appears at a lower α on the longer document. On B-facts a decoy state helps at low α and hurts at high α, at both lengths — but it turns costly near α ≈ 2 on a 600-token document and near α ≈ 0.8 at 50,000. A state accumulated over 50,000 tokens needs less amplification before it starts doing damage.
  • Even a decoy state is faintly positive on A-facts, at every α. mismatched − cold runs +0.005 to +0.219 across α = 1–8 — positive at all eight points, significant at none (best p = 0.115). A consistent direction, not an established effect.
  • Below the context window, pasting the document in still wins. This only competes with long context at lengths that do not fit, and an oracle run — A and B read as one uninterrupted sequence — puts even the scaled transplant at about 2.7% of what having A in context gives you.
  • The effect is weaker than in experiment 1. At an identical 600 tokens, the synthetic looping carrier gives +0.396 nats and real prose gives +0.213, with non-overlapping intervals. Why is not established.

Nothing is planted here. Documents are assembled from unmodified Wikipedia paragraphs; the probes are real sentences taken from A itself. The model gets the opening eight words and is scored on the true continuation, so there is no inserted non-sequitur and no forced choice. The number of probes stops being a design limit: 106 at 600 tokens, 1,600 at 50,000.

It separates two questions experiment 2 could only see combined. Does the state carry information about this particular document? — the estimate is positive at every length, +0.054 down to +0.009 nats/token, but small and uncertain. Per-seed two-sided p runs 0.063, 0.048, 0.352, 0.608, 0.216, so only the 1,500 point clears 0.05, and it does not survive correcting for five lengths. Is transplanting worth doing? — that is warm against cold, because the alternative to a transplant is no transplant, not an irrelevant one. It is positive at 600 and 1,500 and negative from 4,000 on, reaching −0.253 at 50K.

The two must not be conflated. warm − mismatched compares two states that both pay the cost of being carried, so it can only establish document-specificity. The generic cost itself is mismatched − cold, which grows from −0.016 to −0.262 and is what drags the benefit under water.

What this establishes

  • The cost is the solid finding. warm − cold at 50K is −0.253 nats/token with t = −4.46 and 8 of 8 seeds agreeing. By contrast only the 1,500-token point clears the nominal two-sided 5% per-seed test for warm − mismatched (p = 0.048), and it does not survive correcting for five lengths; 600 is p = 0.063. The evidence that carrying a state hurts is much stronger than any evidence that it helps.
  • Specificity is uncertain; the long-document cost is not. warm stays above mismatched at every length including 50K, but the gap is small and clears a per-seed test at only one length (1,500, p = 0.048, which does not survive a five-length correction). Treat it as a consistent direction, not an established effect. But that is a statement about what the state contains, not about whether carrying it is worthwhile: both conditions pay the same cost, so the comparison is between two burdened options. Against no state at all, the transplant is a loss from 4,000 tokens on.
  • No depth effect here. Sorting probes by position in A gives no consistent gradient at any length, which does not reproduce experiment 1's primacy result. Either that finding was specific to planted facts, or it does not survive real prose.
  • B-probes are a ceiling. Loss is about −0.01 nats/token because the sentences come from the document sitting in context — the model is close to copying. Detecting damage there needs a task with headroom.
